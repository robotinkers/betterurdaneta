## History

Urdaneta was founded on January 8, 1858 by Pangasinenses and Ilocanos who wanted closer ties and unity. It is named after Fr. Andrés de Urdaneta, an aide to Miguel López de Legazpi, a soldier, navigator, cosmographer and evangelist. He helped establish the first Spanish settlement on Cebu in 1565 and helped open the trade route between Mexico and the Philippines.

In 1998, Congress passed Republic Act No. 8480, which converted the Municipality of Urdaneta into a component city of Pangasinan. The bill was sponsored by Rep. Amadeo R. Perez Jr., then representing the 5th district. A plebiscite on March 21, 1998 ratified the law, with 26,222 votes in favor and 142 against, and Urdaneta was proclaimed a city.

## Climate

Urdaneta has a tropical monsoon climate. The wet season runs from June to October, and the drier months run from November to May. Daily highs are generally 30 to 34 °C and lows 22 to 25 °C. Rainfall is heaviest in August and lightest in January, and humidity stays high all year.

## Economy

Urdaneta is a gateway to northern Luzon and sits in the central part of eastern Pangasinan. The city produces rice, vegetables and noodles. It has a "Bagsakan" (trading post) where fruits and vegetables are dropped off for sale, and it is home to the largest livestock (cattle) market in northern Luzon. The city also has many banks and financial institutions.

The Sanctuary I.T. Building on MacArthur Highway in Barangay Nancayasan is the only PEZA-proclaimed I.T. building in Pangasinan.

## Festivals

- **Dumayo Festival (March 18 to 31):** free mass wedding, bloodletting, tree planting, a jobs fair and a fun run.
- **City fiesta (December 1 to 8):** held in honor of the city's patron, Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, with a parade, drum and lyre competition, a coronation night and a carnival. The feast day is December 8.
